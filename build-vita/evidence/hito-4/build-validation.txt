Hardware finding (first attempt): the normal probe failed on every launch with
`FAIL memory_block name=azahar-fcram bytes=134217728 code=0x80024302`
(SCE_KERNEL_ERROR_NO_FREE_PHYSICAL_PAGE) followed by `FAIL memory_regions` and `RESULT FAIL`. The
probe's first version additionally built one extra Memory::MemorySystem over a
sceKernelAllocMemBlock-backed environment (MemblockEnvironment) to demonstrate the "separate the
allocations" requirement live, on top of a 160 MiB newlib heap. _newlib_heap_size_user reserves its
full size as one memblock the instant the process starts, for the process's entire lifetime,
whether or not anything has actually been malloc'd from it - not a lazily-grown limit - so the
160 MiB heap plus that demonstration's own ~134.5 MiB request needed ~294.5 MiB against the Vita's
~247 MiB measured user-RAM pool. Caught cleanly by this milestone's own
IsInitialized()/GetFailedItem() seam (a controlled FAIL, not a crash), but a design error
nonetheless: MemblockEnvironment was removed rather than shrinking the heap further, since the
corpus's own heap-based Memory::MemorySystem instances already need close to the full heap by
themselves.

Hardware finding (second attempt): after that fix, one launch appeared unresponsive to START for
long enough to look hung; the recovered boot.log had stopped at "PASS logger" with no further
record. The corpus's own groups (up to two full ~134.5 MiB Memory::MemorySystem constructions in
load_release_cycles alone, each repeating the whole Hito 3 sequence at ~2.85 s per the retained
Hito 3 evidence, plus one more each in region_accounting and oom_recovery) run entirely before the
probe ever reads the controller, and had simply not finished yet. Fixed by dropping
load_release_cycles from three cycles to two (mathematically equivalent - the desktop reference's
load_release_cycles signature, 0x17E0B0FE, is unchanged, since the final signature depends only on
the first cycle's result plus two pass/match booleans) and by logging a `PASS memory_progress`
line after each of the five groups plus a `PASS memory_cycle_progress` line after each
load_release_cycles cycle, so a slow-but-working run is now distinguishable in boot.log from a
stuck one. The retained boot-normal.log above is from the corrected build and shows the corpus
completing in ~10.8 s.
